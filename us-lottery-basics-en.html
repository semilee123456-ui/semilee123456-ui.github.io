So what actually is the US lottery?

For anyone who's brought this up in conversation and gotten a blank stare - here's the real beginner's explanation. What Powerball and Mega Millions are, how they work, how bad the odds really are, and why people play anyway.

1. The US lottery = Powerball + Mega Millions

Individual US states run their own lotteries too, but "the US lottery" usually refers to these two massive games sold jointly across almost every state.

Both work the same basic way - pick 5 numbers plus one special number - which is why this site always covers them side by side.

2. How do you actually play?

  • Pick 5 numbers plus one special number (Powerball/Mega Ball) within the allowed range - or let the terminal pick randomly for you (Quick Pick)
  • The draw happens on a fixed schedule (broadcast live / published online)
  • Prize tiers pay out based on how many numbers you match; matching all 5 plus the special number wins the jackpot
  • If nobody wins the jackpot, it rolls over to the next drawing and keeps growing - that's how you get the record-breaking jackpots you see in the news
💡 A single Powerball ticket costs $2; Mega Millions costs $5 (after its 2025 overhaul). Either way, you're essentially buying a shot at a life-changing outcome for pocket change.

That's over 35 times lower than Korea's own Lotto. Looked at purely as "a way to grow your money," it makes no rational sense.

4. So why does anyone actually play?

The math says don't, but the reason it's such a big deal (in the US and worldwide) is the sheer size of the jackpot. Korean Lotto jackpots typically run in the low billions of won; US lottery jackpots regularly clear $1 billion. Even at terrible odds, "this one ticket could completely change my life" is a powerful draw - and at a couple of dollars a ticket, plenty of people treat it as cheap entertainment rather than a financial decision.

Ticket sales reliably spike whenever a jackpot gets especially large - a pattern that says the emotional pull of "maybe this time" tends to outweigh the actual math for a lot of players.

5. Can I actually buy a ticket from outside the US?

Officially, tickets are only valid when purchased in person at a licensed retailer inside the US (gas stations, convenience stores, etc.). Some online services claim to buy tickets on your behalf from abroad, but their legal standing is often unclear and some carry real fraud risk - approach them with caution.

6. If you did win, how much tax would you actually pay?

The announced jackpot is never what lands in your account. Taking the lump sum cuts it to roughly 45-60% of the announced amount right away, and then US withholding (30% for nonresidents) and any tax owed in your home country apply on top of that. For a lot of winners, what's actually left is somewhere around 40% of the number in the headline.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between Powerball and Mega Millions?

Both are massive multi-state lotteries sold across nearly all of the US. They differ in the number ranges you pick from and the draw days (Powerball: Mon/Wed/Sat, Mega Millions: Tue/Fri). Minimum jackpots and exact odds differ slightly, but the core idea is the same - both regularly produce jackpots over $1 billion.

Are the odds really that low?

Yes. The odds of winning the Powerball jackpot are about 1 in 292.2 million. That's far lower than your lifetime odds of being struck by lightning (roughly 1 in 10,000), and over 35 times lower than winning South Korea's Lotto 6/45 jackpot (about 1 in 8.14 million).

If the odds are this bad, why does anyone play?

Purely on the math, playing isn't rational - but US lottery jackpots are unusually large (routinely over $1 billion), so a ticket is really buying the chance at a single life-changing outcome. At just a couple of dollars a ticket, many people treat it as low-stakes entertainment rather than an investment.

Can I buy US lottery tickets from outside the US?

Officially, tickets are only valid when bought in person at a licensed retailer in the US. Online services that claim to buy tickets on your behalf from abroad exist, but their legal standing is often unclear and some carry real fraud risk, so caution is warranted.
